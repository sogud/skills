# Source Quality

Use this reference when running `Thesis Audit` stage.

## Source hierarchy

| Rank | Source type | Strength |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Regulatory filing | Strong |
| 2 | Annual report / 10-K / 20-F | Strong |
| 3 | Earnings call | Strong / Medium |
| 4 | Investor presentation | Medium |
| 5 | Company product page | Medium |
| 6 | Customer announcement | Strong / Medium |
| 7 | Patent / technical paper | Medium |
| 8 | Reputable industry report | Medium |
| 9 | Financial media | Medium / Weak |
| 10 | Social media | Weak |

## Claim labels

- **Fact**: directly supported.
- **Inference**: reasoned from facts.
- **Assumption**: needed but not yet proven.
- **Rumor**: weak or social source.

## Evidence strength

| Strength | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strong | Direct primary evidence |
| Medium | Indirect but credible evidence |
| Weak | Thin, vague, or secondary evidence |
| Unknown | No reliable evidence yet |

## Rules

- Social media can start research, but cannot close evidence.
- Market price movement is not evidence of business quality.
- A customer logo is not always proof of revenue.
- A product page is not always proof of scale.
- Management guidance is not the same as realized orders.
- If the evidence is missing, write it plainly.
